Pricing a freelance project

How do I calculate a fixed price for a freelance project?

Break the project into tasks, estimate the hours for each, multiply by your hourly rate, add a contingency buffer for the unknowns, add pass-through expenses, then apply any discount and tax. Not sure what your hourly rate should be? Work it out first with the hourly rate calculator.

How much contingency buffer should I add to a quote?

Many freelancers add 10%–25% on top of the estimated hours. Use the lower end for work you have done many times and the higher end for new clients, vague briefs or new technology. Writing a clear statement of work keeps the scope from growing past your buffer.

How big should the deposit be?

A deposit of 30%–50% before work starts is common. It confirms the client is committed and covers your time if the project is cancelled. Put it in your contract, send a deposit invoice, and if the final payment is late, use the late fee calculator.

Should I show the client my hours?

Not necessarily. Many freelancers send only the fixed price and the deliverables, so the client buys an outcome instead of counting hours. The quote text below hides your hourly rate by default — turn your estimate into a full proposal (or a web design proposal) when you are ready.
