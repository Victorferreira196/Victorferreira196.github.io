Late fees on freelance invoices

How do I calculate a late fee on an invoice?

Count the days the invoice is overdue (minus any grace period), then apply the rate in your contract: a monthly percentage prorated by days, an annual percentage prorated by days, or a flat fee. Add the fee to the unpaid amount to get the new total. Once the client pays, send them a payment receipt.

What is a typical late fee for freelancers?

Many freelancers use 1%–2% per month on the unpaid balance, or a small flat fee. Whatever you choose, write it into your contract and your invoice terms before the work starts.

Can I charge a late fee if it was not in my contract?

It is much harder. Late fees are normally enforceable only when the client agreed to them in advance, and some places cap interest or set statutory rates (the UK, for example, has a statutory interest scheme for business-to-business invoices). Check the rules where you and your client are based. This calculator is not legal advice.

Should I always add the fee?

Not necessarily. Many freelancers mention the fee in the first reminder and waive it if the client pays within a few days — it gets you paid without hurting the relationship.
