            What should a copywriting proposal include?

            The client’s goal, the exact deliverables and quantities (pages, emails, articles), your process from discovery to final copy, how many revision rounds are included, what is not included, the price, deposit, kill fee, timeline, who owns the copy and when, and one clear next step. For other kinds of projects use the general proposal generator; if you also design the site, the web design proposal generator.

            How do copywriters price their work?

            Most freelance copywriters price per deliverable or per project, not per word: a fee per website page, per sales page, per email or per article. Estimate the hours each one takes — research, drafting and revisions — and multiply by your target rate. The rate calculator finds your hourly rate and the project quote calculator turns hours into a fixed price.

            How many revisions should I include?

            Two rounds is a common default. Define a round as one consolidated set of feedback, and bill extra rounds by the hour so feedback stays focused. Write it into a freelance contract or a statement of work.

            What is a kill fee?

            The share of the project price the client pays if they cancel after work has started — often 25–50%. A deposit of the same size covers it, so you never chase it. If the balance is paid late, add a late fee.

            Who owns the copy?

            Usually the client gets full ownership once the final invoice is paid. Until then, the copy stays yours — a strong reason to send the invoice on delivery and a receipt when it is paid.
