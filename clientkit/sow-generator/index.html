Statement of work: common questions

What is a statement of work?

A statement of work (SOW) defines exactly what you will deliver, by when, for how much — and what is not included. It sits next to your contract or proposal and is the document you point to when scope starts to creep.

What is the difference between a SOW and a proposal?

A proposal sells the project; a SOW specifies it. Once the client says yes, the SOW lists deliverables, milestones, acceptance criteria and payment terms both sides agree to. Start with the free proposal generator, then lock the details here.

What should be out of scope?

Anything the client might reasonably assume is included but isn't: extra pages, copywriting, hosting, stock assets, maintenance or more revision rounds. Naming them up front turns "can you just…" into a priced change request.

How do I price the deliverables?

Estimate hours per deliverable and multiply by a rate that covers taxes, costs and unbilled time — the rate calculator gives you that floor. Bill each milestone with the invoice generator.

Is this SOW legally binding?

It becomes part of your agreement once both parties sign it, but it is a template, not legal advice. Have a local lawyer review terms for high-value projects.
